Civil Engineering

Civil engineering is the profession that designs, builds, and maintains the structures and systems that modern communities depend on every day. Roads, bridges, water pipes, dams, airports, and buildings all begin as the work of civil engineers. This page gives a short introduction to the field, its main areas of work, and why it matters.

What Is Civil Engineering?

Civil engineering is one of the oldest engineering fields, dating back to ancient projects such as Roman aqueducts and roads. Today, civil engineers use math, science, and computer modeling to plan projects that are safe, affordable, and long lasting. They work with governments, construction companies, and consulting firms to turn ideas into real structures that people can use.

Main Areas of Civil Engineering

The field includes several specialties. Structural engineers design buildings and bridges that can carry loads and resist wind and earthquakes. Transportation engineers plan highways, transit systems, and airports. Geotechnical engineers study soil and rock to make sure foundations are stable. Water resources and environmental engineers manage drinking water, stormwater, wastewater, and flood protection.

Skills and Career Path

Civil engineers need strong math and problem-solving skills, along with clear communication and teamwork. Most start with a bachelor's degree in civil engineering, gain experience working under a licensed engineer, and then earn a Professional Engineer (PE) license. A career can lead to roles in design, construction management, public works, or running an engineering company.
